How the clash rule works. A colour note (9, 11 or 13) that sits a half step above a chord tone rubs against it and is marked as one to avoid: the 11 over a major 3rd, the ♭9 over a minor chord's root, the ♭13 over a perfect 5th. Dominant 7 chords are the exception for ♭9 and ♭13, which are their classic tensions.

Spelling follows the one-letter-per-degree rule, so you will see E♯ in F♯ major and F𝄪 in G♯ harmonic minor. Auto picks the key name with fewer accidentals.
